## Why vectors matter

A scalar records magnitude. A vector records both magnitude and direction. Position, velocity, acceleration, force, and momentum are therefore naturally represented as vectors.

In Cartesian coordinates, a three-dimensional vector can be written as

$$
\mathbf{v} = v_x\mathbf{\hat{i}} + v_y\mathbf{\hat{j}} + v_z\mathbf{\hat{k}}.
$$

Its magnitude is

$$
\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2 + v_z^2}.
$$

## Components and direction

For a two-dimensional vector with magnitude $v$ making an angle $\theta$ with the positive $x$-axis,

$$
v_x = v\cos\theta,
\qquad
v_y = v\sin\theta.
$$

The component form is useful because vector addition becomes ordinary arithmetic:

$$
\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}
= (a_x+b_x)\mathbf{\hat{i}} + (a_y+b_y)\mathbf{\hat{j}}.
$$

## A small Python representation

```python
from math import hypot

def magnitude(x: float, y: float) -> float:
    """Return the magnitude of a two-dimensional vector."""
    return hypot(x, y)

print(magnitude(3, 4))  # 5.0
```
