## Describing position

The position of an object moving along a straight line can be represented by a function $x(t)$. Its displacement between two times is

$$
\Delta x = x(t_2) - x(t_1)
$$

Displacement records the net change in position, so it can be positive, negative, or zero even when the object has travelled some distance.

## Velocity and acceleration

Instantaneous velocity is the rate at which position changes:

$$
v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt}
$$

Acceleration measures the corresponding rate of change of velocity:

$$
a(t) = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}
$$

For constant acceleration, integrating these relations gives

$$
v(t) = v_0 + at
\qquad
x(t) = x_0 + v_0t + \frac{1}{2}at^2
$$

## A small numerical example

```python
def position(t: float, x0: float = 0, v0: float = 4, a: float = 2) -> float:
    """Position at time t for motion with constant acceleration."""
    return x0 + v0 * t + 0.5 * a * t**2

print(position(3))  # 21.0
```
